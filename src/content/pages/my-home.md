This page covers what makes up your unit, who is responsible for what, and how to look after the appliances and equipment that came with it. For changes to the structure or fittings, see [Alterations](/my-home/alterations).

## Boundaries

### What is "my home"?

What are the boundaries between _your_ property and your neighbour's or a common area? The [Unit Titles Act 2001 (ACT)](https://www.legislation.act.gov.au/View/a/2001-16/current/html/2001-16.html) defines these.

![Boundary diagram from the Unit Titles Act 2001 (ACT)](../../assets/images/my-home/boundaries-diagram.jpg)

### Terrace units: where is the boundary for my outside walls?

A terrace unit's front wall spans two levels. The ground level is entirely inside your courtyard, so both sides of the wall are _your_ responsibility. The upper level is _not_ inside your courtyard --- it is above it --- so its outside is common property, just like the walls of the units above.

### Can I sit a pot plant on the wall between my unit and my neighbour's?

You could if your pot fits on your half of the wall, but doing so is:

- unlikely if the wall is narrow
- hazardous (wind can dislodge it), even if the neighbour consents to the encroachment.

This is a reminder to consider hazards as well as boundaries.

### Where are the boundaries for my water, sewerage and drainage pipes?

To be confirmed --- check the Independent's Guide to Owner v OC Responsibilities.

## Warranty

### What is my unit's warranty?

In the ACT, unit owners have statutory warranties:

- six years for structural elements
- two years for non-structural elements such as plumbing, electrical work, and finishes.

Since Southport received its Certificates of Occupancy in 2017, both warranties have expired. For more details, see [this article by Minter Ellison](https://constructionlawmadeeasy.com/construction-law/chapter-14/australian-capital-territory/#:~:text=The%20statutory%20warranties%20expire%20six,required%20by%20the%20statutory%20warranties.).

Fortunately, before the structural warranty expired, Grady Strata engaged engineers to identify defects. Their report --- _Leo Meng & Associates --- Remedial Engineering Report_ (added 07/04/2025) --- is available on [Grady's portal](https://gradystrata.my.smata.com). Grady is pursuing GEOCON to rectify the defects identified in that report, along with others.

### What is a defect?

The answer is not always obvious. The [NSW Guide to Standards and Tolerances 2017](https://www.fairtrading.nsw.gov.au/housing-and-property/building-and-renovating/after-you-build-or-renovate/guide-to-standards-and-tolerances) provides guidance that is also recognised in the ACT. Although the warranty on your unit's original construction has expired, this guide can still help you during an alteration.

### Who should I notify about a defect?

Even if the defect isn't covered by warranty, please still report it in case it could affect other units or common property. Notify [Grady](/contact) or the [Building Manager](/contact).

<summary>Exhaust fans</summary>

![Bathroom exhaust fan](../../assets/images/my-home/exhaust-fan.jpg)

Your bathroom and laundry have exhaust fans in their ceilings. The bathroom fan switches with the light; the laundry fan has a separate switch.

The fans are so quiet you need to listen carefully to tell if they're working. They gradually slow over several years and can eventually stop, and a steamy bathroom risks developing mould --- so it's worth checking.

A working fan should easily hold a sheet of paper:

![Working exhaust fan holding a sheet of paper](../../assets/images/my-home/exhaust-fan-working.jpg)

If the suction struggles to hold even a single ply of tissue paper, the fan is no longer functioning correctly (keep your hand ready to stop the tissue from being sucked in). It won't be long before it stops altogether.

![Failing exhaust fan barely holding tissue paper](../../assets/images/my-home/exhaust-fan-failing.jpg)

Get an electrician to replace a dead or dying fan before mould has a chance to develop.

</details>

<details class="appliance">
<summary>Downlights</summary>

![LED downlight removed from ceiling](../../assets/images/my-home/downlight.png)

You don't need an electrician to replace an LED downlight --- it simply plugs into a standard 240 V socket in the ceiling. Always follow the instructions that came with your new downlight, but here is a general guide:

1. Switch off the downlight.
2. Use a small step ladder so you can comfortably reach the ceiling.
3. Carefully prise the downlight's trim away from the ceiling until you can get your fingers behind it.
4. Gently and evenly pull the downlight down until you see the two spring-loaded retaining arms.
5. Squeeze the retaining arms together and continue to withdraw the downlight. (Watch your fingers --- the retaining arms are as dangerous as a mousetrap.)
6. Carefully relax the retaining arms once they clear the hole.
7. Slowly pull the downlight and its cord down until you see the plug and socket above the hole.
8. Hold the socket and unplug the downlight.
9. Buy a new downlight with a similar diameter (90--92 mm), face (flush), wattage, colour, colour temperature, and finish. Possible replacements include the Arlec ALD295RV and Click 22619 (around $10 from Bunnings).
10. If the new downlight's colour temperature is adjustable, set it to match your previous one or experiment. (Higher temperature = whiter light.)
11. Install by reversing the steps. Ensure no ceiling insulation covers the downlight, and again, watch your fingers.
12. Switch on your new downlight.

**Warning:** these steps are only a guide. Follow the instructions that came with your new downlight, and call an electrician if you aren't confident.

</details>

<details class="appliance">
<summary>Outdoor tiles</summary>

![Outdoor balcony tiles](../../assets/images/my-home/outdoor-tiles.jpg)

**How do I wash the tiles on my balcony without wetting my neighbours' balconies and courtyards below?** One resident's suggestion:

1. Pour water sparingly on the stains.
2. Scrub the stains.
3. Squeegee the water off the tiles into the gaps around them.
4. Repeat using water mixed with disinfectant.

If that doesn't work, try [Chemtech Brick Tile and Paver Cleaner](https://www.bunnings.com.au/chemtech-1l-brick-tile-and-paver-cleaner_p0964678) (1 L and 5 L from Bunnings) --- it will even remove the white deposits that often form under pot plants.

**Can I coat the tiles to make them easier to clean?** Yes --- the Building Manager is considering this for some common-area tiles. When choosing a coating, ask about its slip resistance when applied to your tiles. (The Australian Standard is AS 4663, _Slip resistance measurement of existing pedestrian surfaces_.)

**How do I replace a cracked or broken tile?**

1. Buy a new tile (see Exterior finishes above).
2. Make or borrow a tile lifter (the [Building Manager](/contact) may have one).
3. Use the tile lifter to lift and remove the tile.
4. Use the tile lifter to gently lower and lay the new tile (watch your fingers).
5. Check the new tile's stability (does it rock when you stand on any corner?) and evenness.
6. Analyse which corners need a thicker or thinner shim.
7. Lift the corners and add or remove shims as needed.
8. Repeat until the tile is stable and even.

If a tile isn't cracked but is unstable or uneven, follow steps 6 to 8.

</details>

<summary>External wall vents</summary>

![External wall vent](../../assets/images/my-home/external-wall-vent.jpg)

**How do I remove an external vent?** Carefully prise it out from the wall using a flat-bladed screwdriver.

**How do I reinstall it?** Press it back evenly and carefully.

**What if one side won't engage?** The housing may only be screwed in on one side because there was no wall stud to screw into on the other. Pressing the vent on the unscrewed side then pushes the housing into the wall rather than onto the clips.

![Wall vent housing where one side has no screw and won't engage the clip](../../assets/images/my-home/wall-vent-clip-issue.jpg)

One solution is to make and fit simple brackets to retain the unscrewed side of the housing:

![Brackets fitted to retain the wall vent housing](../../assets/images/my-home/wall-vent-bracket.jpg)

## Renting your unit

### Can I rent my unit?

![House with "for rent" sign](../../assets/images/my-home/renting-unit.png)

Yes, of course. We want Southport to be as great for investing as it is for living. We kindly ask that you or your agent inform your tenants of our [rules](/rules), because we will hold you responsible.

### Can I rent my unit as short-stay accommodation?

Yes. As well as Canberra's general appeal to family visitors and tourists, Southport's closeness to two large government offices makes it particularly attractive to business travellers. We only ask that you:

- ask our Strata Manager (see [Contact](/contact)) for permission before installing a lock box on the common property
- let your guests know about our [rules](/rules)
- give your guests clear instructions on how to find your carpark, as some of the numbering is random.

We hope our website helps your guests enjoy their stay.

**Tip:** our residents include cleaners (and even a chef for your guests). See the "Southport Tuggeranong Residents" Facebook Group.

### Can I rent my carpark?

![Carpark with oil-drip mats](../../assets/images/my-home/carpark.jpg)

Yes. For security, please only rent it to other residents --- you could try advertising on the "Southport Tuggeranong Residents" Facebook Group.

It is your responsibility to:

- consider the taxation implications, including income tax and capital gains tax
- keep the space clean
- ask your renter to move their car for garage cleaning or other maintenance.

We recommend you:

- clarify whether the rent covers your storage cage (your choice)
- take photos of the car space and neighbouring storage shed (if present) to help resolve disputes about cleanliness or damage
- ask your renter to leave enough of a gap for you to open the door of your storage cage
- protect the floor from oil drips (the purpose of the black plastic Bunnings mats in the photo above).
