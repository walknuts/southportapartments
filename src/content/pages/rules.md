## Current rules

These rules:

- were approved by the AGM on 22 October 2022
- took effect 28 days later because the approval was a 'reduced quorum decision'
- were registered with the Land Titles Office on 23 January 2023.

They were a major rewrite renumbered to align with the Default Rules of the *Unit Titles (Management) Regulation 2011*. The rewrite added nine new rules drawn from Grady Strata's experience.

See the [Explanatory memorandum](/downloads/explanatory-memorandum-for-rules-update-from-2022-agm-notice.pdf) for details.

### Notes on the current rules

**Definitions apply to all rules.** The Definitions section applies to *all* our rules, not only the default rules as the heading might suggest.

**Numbering starts at '1' in each section.** This is an inefficient legacy from the Default Rules in the [Unit Titles (Management) Regulation 2011 (ACT)](https://www.legislation.act.gov.au/View/sl/2011-39/current/html/2011-39.html). Despite the inefficiency, consistency with the Default Rules is more important.

**'Shall' versus 'must'.** Section 1.5(1)(d) is one example of our rules using 'shall' instead of 'must', the preferred regulatory practice according to section 146(2) of the [Legislation Act 2001 (ACT)](https://www.legislation.act.gov.au/View/a/2001-14/current/html/2001-14.html). Should we globally replace 'shall' with 'must' for our next amendment?

### Amendments approved since 2022

The 2024 AGM voted to amend rule 1.7(2)(vi) to read:

> Smoking, vaping and the use of e-cigarettes is not permitted within common property including foyers, lifts, fire escape stairwells, the garage and in front of any external entry door to the building up to 10m from the entry doors.

The 2023 AGM voted to reintroduce 4.vii from the old rules as (5) to rule 1.9:

> Residents who smoke on their private property including balconies are requested to be considerate of others in the vicinity.

The AGM also voted to add this Note to the new (5) of rule 1.9:

> Note: Smokers in 'Stage 1' (Units 1 to 176) should be aware that their 'vicinity' includes units that require extra consideration. They are the units the developer marketed as 'cross-ventilated' because they were specially designed to draw a 'flowing breeze' from the building's aerodynamics.
>
> Unfortunately, it also means a 'cross-ventilated' unit is more prone to ingesting smoke from neighbours.

The 2024 AGM voted to amend rule 1.19(1)(e) to read:

> Unit Owners, Occupiers and their invitees must not smoke, vape, use e-cigarettes or use or consume any prohibited drug in or about the Recreational Facilities.

On 20 July 2025, the EC decided not to include Rule 1.20(5) in the package of rules to submit to the OC at the 2025 AGM. Until then, the rule remains in force.

The original rules registered with the Land Titles Office are available on request.

## Enforcement and dispute resolution

Reasonable rules should foster willing compliance. However...

### Rule infringement notice

If compliance with a rule cannot be agreed upon, the EC may issue a rule infringement notice to the alleged offender under section 109 of the [Unit Titles (Management) Act 2011 (ACT)](https://www.legislation.act.gov.au/View/a/2011-41/current/html/2011-41.html).

Failure to comply with a rule infringement notice is an offence that can attract a fine, as s 110 explains.

If a unit owner's non-compliance with the rules affects another owner, the second owner may request that the EC issue a rule infringement notice to the first owner, as s 111 explains.

### ACAT orders

The [ACT Civil & Administrative Tribunal (ACAT)](https://www.acat.act.gov.au) can issue orders:

- if an owners corporation needs greater authority to enforce a rule, or
- if an owner disputes a rule or its enforcement.

The ACAT can hear civil cases for claims up to $25,000.

Part 8 of the [Unit Titles (Management) Act 2011](https://www.legislation.act.gov.au/View/a/2011-41/current/html/2011-41.html) has more details on these and other disputes.
