### A policy on policy

**Why this policy?** We need a policy on policy to remind us of the basics of sound policy.

**What are the basics?** The Australian Public Service Academy outlines them. For more, see:

- [The APS Academy's website](https://www.apsacademy.gov.au/aps-craft/strategy-policy-evaluation/delivering-great-policy)
- [The Australian Policy Handbook, 7th Edition, published by Routledge](https://www.routledge.com/The-Australian-Policy-Handbook-A-Practical-Guide-to-the-Policymaking-Process/Althaus-Ball-Bridgman-Davis-Threlfall/p/book/9781032399201).

**Is government policy relevant to strata?** Although we are a 'corporation', our aim is not to generate profit but to provide services to our constituents, the owners, similar to good government. To do this fairly, effectively, and efficiently, sound policies are essential, including policies for regulation and enforcement, much like a government.

**How can we keep our policies simple and focused?** By structuring them as FAQs, with the first question for each policy being 'Why this policy?'

**Status:** unapproved draft.

### EC membership

**Why this policy?** To help us apply the legislative requirements in the UTMA to our EC.

**How does Southport's being a 'mixed use units plan' affect our EC membership?** Section 39A(2) of the [Unit Titles (Management) Act 2011](https://www.legislation.act.gov.au/View/a/2011-41/current/html/2011-41.html) requires our EC membership to include:

- at least one owner of a residential unit; and
- at least one shop owner.

Our practical interpretation is that we have complied with this requirement if:

- we specifically invite owners to join the EC, whether at the AGM or filling a casual vacancy; and
- we elect at least one shop owner who volunteers.

We cannot *compel* a shop owner to apply.

**How should we induct new members?** Our induction should include:

- introductions to Grady's people, processes and tools
- training in the [Unit Titles (Management) Act 2011](https://www.legislation.act.gov.au/View/a/2011-41/current/html/2011-41.html)
- universal fob access
- a corporate email address
- TK.

**Status:** unapproved draft.

### Style guide

**Why this policy?** We need a Style Guide to project 'Southport' as a consistent, professional 'brand' to the market, visitors and residents.

**Origin:** the style GEOCON used in their early marketing of Southport, in particular the 2015 brochure.

**Wordmark:** we have not yet been able to identify the font. It is not Archive Modern II Open (as GEOCON suggested on 10 July 2025), nor Paganini Open (as Screenmakers suggested on 2 July 2025), nor Chevalier. The closest alternatives so far are Chevalier, Ecuyer Dax and Currency Regular.

**Text:** On 10 July 2025, GEOCON said the fonts in the 2015 brochure were Laro Soft SemiBold (headings) and Laro Soft Regular (body). Our forensic analysis instead identified:

- titles: Gotham Bold
- headings: Gotham Medium
- body text: Gotham Book.

Monotype's Gotham fonts are affordable for personal use but not for a non-profit website. Almost identical and free is [Metropolis](https://www.learnui.design/blog/gotham-similar-fonts.html), which we adopted in July 2025, starting with this website, the new entrance signs, and the new unit-number signs on entrance doors.

**Colours:** the colours from GEOCON's 2015 brochure are also the colours of this website:

- dark background and text: dark grey, hex #696770
- light background: 'earthy white', hex #F6F1E4.

**Writing style:**

- 'plain English'
- friendly
- personal (first person).

Write instructions in the active voice and the imperative mood, rather than the passive voice and the indicative mood.

**Notices in the lift and elsewhere:** minimum font size TK; word count not to exceed TK so the average reader can read it in less than TK seconds.

### Driveway parking

**Why this policy?** Driveway parking in violation of our signs is common. This misbehaviour reduces the amenity and safety for others. The purpose of this policy is to guide our enforcement in a manner that is effective, practical, and lawful.

**Could we leave enforcement to the ACT's Parking Inspectors?** No, because our driveway is private property.

**What have we tried?**

- polite reminders under the windscreen wipers of offending vehicles
- removable bollards
- hard-to-remove stickers.

**Are the stickers lawful?** We assume so because Grady Strata recommended them. The question arises because 'damaging property' is an offence under s 403 of the [Criminal Code 2002 (ACT)](https://www.legislation.act.gov.au/View/a/2002-51/current/html/2002-51.html), noting that s 400(d) defines 'damage' to include defacing. We trust Grady Strata's advice that the sticker's removability, despite the intentional difficulty, would be a reasonable defence.

**Status:** unapproved draft.

### Approving spending

**Why this policy?** The UTMA is neither concise nor complete.

**Strata Manager's advice.** On 5 May 2025, Jordan Williams asked Michael Grady:

> What expenditure can be approved by the EC without reference to an AGM?

Michael's reply (verbatim):

> In the ACT there is [sic] distinctly less spending controls on a Committee than in other states. Given the lack of detailed legislated controls, Committees are tended to mind the following provisions within the Act:
>
> - s 77 and S88, which prescribe relevantly that:
>   - the general fund (admin fund) can only be used if the payments are approved within the general fund budget, or otherwise approved by ordinary resolution.
>   - the sinking fund can only be used if payments are in accordance with the sinking fund plan.
> - S35(3)(b), which prescribes that the Committee must exercise its functions as it considers appropriate in the absence of a resolution. Whilst this power appears fairly broad, it is counteracted by the Act insofar as it prescribes a decision only to the Owners Corporation and not anybody else.
>
> The Code of Conduct for EC Members whereby EC Members must ensure they act in the best interests of the Owners Corporation.
>
> The Act doesn't provide for overspending limits, leaving a vagary as to whether the Executive Committee can overspend (and by how much) a particular budget line item, provided the line item exists somewhere within the budget. If each Owners Corporation were to adopt the position that it could not overspend any line item during a year, then Owners Corporations (and by extension the Committee) would be unnecessarily hamstrung in their ability to meet their obligations, which is primarily to maintain the Common Property.
>
> Most large buildings will typically operate within the following parameters when considering various items:
>
> - For capital expenditures that sit within the Sinking Fund, are urgent or necessary within a particularly short time period, but are not budgeted within that particular year --- bring forward the expense as necessary and report on same at the AGM. Otherwise spend per the plan, or delay expenses from expenditure if they are unnecessary.
> - For admin fund items where over expenditure is likely to occur but it is in the interests of the Owners Corporation --- as long as the expenditure amount will not result in a negative year-balance within the Admin Fund and doesn't exceed ~20% (variable by UP) of the budgeted total or require additional contributions --- proceed and report on that expenditure the following AGM.
> - For any improvements to the building (EV charging, spa, new gardens, etc.) that are not budgeted and are considered major pieces of work, seek approval from an AGM via a specific motion.
>
> More broadly, if an Owners Corporation is not in agreement with the Committee's policies with respect to use of Owners Corporations funds, it has the option to place controls on expenditure, or elect a differently constituted Committee.

**Summary of Grady's advice:**

- strictly, an EC can only spend what the OC has approved in the budget or through a special resolution
- practically, an EC can exercise its judgment on how much overspend it believes the OC will retrospectively approve at the next AGM
- approval becomes more likely if the EC can offset an overspend on one item with an underspend on another, at least for the Admin fund.

**Status:** unapproved draft.

### Lighting

**Why this policy?** To inform lighting improvements and maintenance, mainly to ensure a consistent look and feel for our lighting throughout Southport.

**Colour temperature.** Warm white for hallways:

- [the Lighting Council of Australia defines warm white as 2700K to 3000K](https://lightingcouncil.com.au/led-buyers-guide/index.html)
- adds coziness to Southport's otherwise austere architecture
- Paul Hattersley agreed to implement this in an email on 3 July 2025.

Cool white elsewhere (3200K to 4500K), [as the Lighting Council of Australia recommends](https://www.lightingstyle.com.au/guide-to-lighting-colour-temperature.html):

- security lighting
- carparks
- pool area.

We should try not to mix colour temperatures in the same scene.

**Which lighting technology?** As the opportunities arise, we should progressively upgrade all our lighting to LED for increased efficiency.

**Sensor or 24/7?** Some of our lighting is sensor-activated to conserve electricity (external walkways, carparks). Other lighting is always on (in and around the pool area). It is about balancing:

- the installation and maintenance cost of sensors
- the shortening of lamp life (depends on cycles as well as hours)
- the savings in electricity.

**Status:** unapproved draft.

### Privacy

**Why this policy?** To ensure compliance with our legislative obligations for privacy.

**[The Information Privacy Act 2014 (ACT)](https://www.legislation.act.gov.au/View/a/2014-24/current/html/2014-24.html)** only applies to 'public sector agencies and contracted service providers'. We are neither, so this Act does not apply to us.

**[The Workplace Privacy Act 2011 (ACT)](https://www.legislation.act.gov.au/View/a/2011-4/current/html/2011-4.html)** applies to corporations that employ workers, including contractors. That includes us. TK.

**[The Privacy Act 1988 (Cth)](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A03712/latest/text)** applies to an 'organisation', and s 6C(1)(b) says that includes a 'body corporate'. If the Act does include us, as it seems, we must adhere to the Australian Privacy Principles outlined in Schedule 1.

Worth reading: ['Access to Owners corporation information, its use and privacy issues'](https://www.ocnact.org.au/use-and-privacy-of-owners-corporation-information) from the Owners Corporation Network in the ACT.

**How should we protect the privacy of owners' correspondence?** TK.

**Status:** unapproved draft. We should get professional legal advice before approving it.

### Website

**Why this policy?** To explain the website's purposes, funding, approval and review.

**Purposes:** marketing, management, community, education, training, communication, quality, effectiveness, efficiency, continuity, consistency, transparency, repository.

In their ['Best Practice Guide'](https://www.ocnact.org.au/best-practice-guide), the OCN (ACT) recommend that OCs have their own website, separate from the strata manager's website.

**Funding:** the webmaster has volunteered to pay all the initial and recurring costs for domain registration, email and web hosting, and software. There is no cost to the OC.

**Approval process:** the EC's standard approval process would be impractical given the need to constantly and frequently update the website. A parliamentary mechanism for a similar situation is the *disallowable instrument* --- for example, in section 9(1) of the [Legislation Act 2001 (ACT)](https://www.legislation.act.gov.au/View/a/2001-14/current/html/2001-14.html). Applied to the website:

1. the webmaster publishes and updates the website
2. if an EC member has a concern, they contact the webmaster
3. the webmaster either addresses the concern, or brings it to the EC for arbitration.

**Review process:** the website is a trial, open to review by all stakeholders --- the webmaster, the EC, the OC, the strata and building managers, and visitors, deliverers and contractors.

The OCN (ACT)'s ['Best Practice Guide'](https://www.ocnact.org.au/best-practice-guide) advises looking long-term:

> Owners often need more information about the issues the OC is dealing with and the available solutions than the minutes or general meeting papers provide. As the OC is 'all in it together', providing more information through a letter to all owners, a regular Newsletter, a website managed by the OC itself etc may lead to more disputation in the short term but to better decision making in the longer term.

**What if the webmaster doesn't want to keep running the website?** They could offer the website's files and domain to the EC, the Strata Manager, or a professional website business. If the recipient prefers different software, they could import the content into their own.

**What if the webmaster wanted to keep running the website, but the EC didn't?** If willing, the webmaster could consider running the website independently, after consulting with the other stakeholders. The website could still be helpful even without its EC status, much like the independent Facebook groups.

**Status:** unapproved draft.

### Workers compensation insurance

**Why this policy?** To answer the reasonable question that the Chair of the EC asked Michael Grady on 28 August 2025:

> Why should we pay for workers compensation when the OC doesn't employ any staff? Everything is contracted out.

The question arose because the latest renewal proposal included workers compensation for the first time.

Michael Grady replied:

> Because the law treats some people an OC engages (cleaners, gardeners, Building Managers, etc.) as workers or deemed workers (due to the regularity of attendance) of the Owners Corporation, the OC carries risk of a claim against them if one of them is injured while doing common-property work. The OC doesn't engage any trades directly, but despite this can be the "employer" for workers-comp purposes. As such, regulators require (or strongly expect) a workers-comp policy to be in place. Typically we'd see the employers Workers Comp insurance as the first respondent, but there is a risk that a contractor lets their workers comp elapse for a short period, and if there is an incident at that time the OC may become the first respondent to a claim. For the cost vs. risk mitigation it is typically worth the cost.

**What is the policy?** We should have workers compensation insurance as Grady recommends.

**Status:** unapproved draft.
